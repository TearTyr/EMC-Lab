Governing equations
  • q = n·e, e = 1.602×10−19 C — charge is quantised and conserved
  • F = k·|q₁q₂|/r², added as vectors
  • E = F/|q₀| = kq/r² — the direction rule: push on a + test charge
  • Extra: V = IR, F = qv × B, EMF = −N·dΦ/dt

Quick formula reference

One cheat-sheet table: the lecture’s formulas first, then the Extra ones, with units and what each tells you.

ConceptEquationUnitsWhat it tells you
Quantised chargeq = n·eCCharge comes in whole electron parcels; 1 C = 6.24×1018 of them
Coulomb forceF = k·|q1q2| / r2NInverse square: 2× the distance → ¼ the force; signs pick direction only
SuperpositionF⃗net = F⃗₁ + F⃗₂ + …NForces and fields add as vectors (components), never as plain numbers
Electric fieldE = F/|q₀| = k·q/r2N/CForce per unit positive test charge; E points the way a + charge is pushed
Force in a fieldF = |q₀|·ENA negative charge feels it opposite E
Bohr speedv = √(ke²/(mₑr))m/sCoulomb pull as centripetal force: 2.19×10⁶ m/s at r = 5.29×10⁻¹¹ m
Extra · Ohm’s lawV = I·R, P = VIV, A, Ω, WVoltage drives current against resistance; power is the rate of heating
Extra · networksReq = ΣRᵢ / 1/Req = Σ1/RᵢΩSeries adds; parallel adds conductances (always smaller than the smallest)
Extra · magnetic forceF = q·v × B, r = mv/(|q|B)N, mPerpendicular to v and B → bends paths, never does work
Extra · FaradayEMF = −N·dΦ/dt, Φ = B·A·cosθV, WbOnly a changing flux induces; the minus is Lenz’s law
